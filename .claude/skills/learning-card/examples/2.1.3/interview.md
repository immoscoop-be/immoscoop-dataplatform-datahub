## 1. The reader's question

**Asked:** Who asked you this recently, and how did they put it?

**Answer:** Nobody asked it yet; the side entrance: what should they start asking? "Sessions went up, why didn't the leads?", after a campaign that was steered on sessions.

**In the card:** Why now, the quote and who says it; the situation.

## 2. What went wrong

**Asked:** What went wrong, concretely? One metric from our dashboards, or one sentence a colleague said.

**Answer:** Sessions are leading, generated leads are lagging. A target on sessions alone makes sessions the goal, and sessions are easy to buy without buying leads. The misconception: more sessions means more leads, in the same week.

**In the card:** Core idea, "At Immoscoop"; See it, with example figures (4 leads per 100 sessions, a campaign of +40% sessions); the wrong answers of the questions.

## 3. The aha

**Asked:** What is the one thing that, once someone sees it, makes the rest obvious?

**Answer:** Lagging = leading × factor, after a delay.

**In the card:** Core idea, the rule.

## 4. The everyday anchor

**Asked:** Which everyday thing behaves exactly like this?

**Answer:** A shop: people through the door are the early number, people who buy the late one. Some share of visitors buys, and in a real-estate shop weeks after the visit. Free coffee at the door raises the counter, not the sales. Tested: the shop carries the factor (the share that buys) and the delay (weeks later). Claude's seed-and-harvest anchor was turned down.

**In the card:** Core idea, "Like a shop".

## 5. Never again, ask first

**Asked:** What may a colleague never do again after this card, and what do they ask first from now on?

**Answer:** Never set a target on the leading indicator alone. Ask first: what's the factor, and what's the lead time?

**In the card:** You can now (the question to ask first); the never-again is the wrong answer of the second question.
