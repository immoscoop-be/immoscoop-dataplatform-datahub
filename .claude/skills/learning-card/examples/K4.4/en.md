# Why now

> These figures don't match the dashboard. I'll just fix them in my Excel.
> — A colleague from sales, before a meeting

Nobody hears about it: not the Owner of the figure, and not the next colleague who reads the same figure and doubts it too. Half the time the figure was right and the reading was not.

Promise: what to do when a figure looks wrong, and whom to tell.

# Core idea

A figure that looks wrong starts with one question, **do I understand it?**, and climbs a ladder: read its definition, ask its Owner, then raise a Signal.

## Like a smoke alarm

You smell smoke, so it is yours to raise. But you check for burnt toast before you call the fire brigade. Either way you say something, because the next person will smell it too.

## At Immoscoop

Two names that look alike, `leads` and `creditLead`, get read as the same thing, and the figures don't add up. "The data is wrong" goes round in Slack, or gets fixed quietly in Excel. The definition of each name would have shown that they count different things.

# Check your understanding

## A figure on the monthly report looks too low to you. What do you do first?

- [ ] Correct it in your Excel and move on
- [x] Read the definition of the figure, and ask its Owner what it does not answer
- [ ] Post in Slack that the data is wrong

Why: most figures that look wrong are read wrong: two names that look alike, or another period. The definition settles that in a minute, and the Owner answers what it does not.

## You asked the Owner, and the figure really does seem off. Where do you say so?

- [ ] In a private message to the Owner, so nobody is embarrassed
- [x] In a Signal on the Resource, where the next colleague sees it too
- [ ] Nowhere: someone else will notice

Why: a Signal is never private, so whoever reads the figure next sees the doubt and its answer. A private message helps one person, once.

## A colleague wonders whether their question about a figure is a stupid one. What holds?

- [x] If it is unclear to them, it is unclear to others too: asking it helps everyone
- [ ] Only the Data Team asks questions about figures
- [ ] Questions slow the Data Team down

Why: a question asked in the open is answered once, for everyone who reads the Resource after. There is no such thing as a bad question about a figure.

# You can now

check whether you understand a figure before you call it wrong, and raise a Signal on its Resource when it still looks off.
