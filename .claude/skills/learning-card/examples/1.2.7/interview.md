## 1. The reader's question

**Asked:** Who asked you this recently, and how did they put it?

**Answer:** The test round gave the question in data words: "are we counting what a thing is, or what happened to it?". The card needs the colleague's own sentence, so Claude asked again; "How many listings did we have in October?", for a monthly report, is the sentence the author confirms or replaces.

**In the card:** Why now, the quote and who says it.

## 2. What went wrong

**Asked:** What went wrong, concretely? One metric from our dashboards, or one sentence a colleague said.

**Answer:** First theory ("a state and an event are conceptually different", "one row per thing against one row per happening"); Claude pushed back twice for a figure. Then: properties created in a month is an event you add up; properties online on a day is a state you snapshot; rent versus sale is an attribute you slice both by. Add up thirty daily snapshots and you have counted every property thirty times.

**In the card:** Core idea, "At Immoscoop"; the situation of Why now; See it; the wrong answer "8,400" of the second question.

## 3. The aha

**Asked:** What is the one thing that, once someone sees it, makes the rest obvious?

**Answer:** You count events; you take a snapshot of states.

**In the card:** Core idea, the rule, in the words of the plan: a state and a movement.

## 4. The everyday anchor

**Asked:** Which everyday thing behaves exactly like this?

**Answer:** A door: its colour is a state, its opening an event. Tested: "does your door carry the attribute too?" The colour carries it; "how often do red doors open?" is where the two meet.

**In the card:** Core idea, "Like a door".

## 5. Never again, ask first

**Asked:** What may a colleague never do again after this card, and what do they ask first from now on?

**Answer:** Never add up snapshots. Ask first: are we counting behaviour or volume?

**In the card:** You can now, in plainer words (what happened, or how things stand); the wrong answers of the second and third question.
